## `/` Landing

**Hero line (beneath logotype):**
> Clinical decision support built for how care actually happens in Tanzania :  offline, bilingual, and grounded in national guidelines.

**Product snapshot paragraph:**
> ClerQ is a clinical decision support system for frontline clinicians, built directly on Tanzania's Standard Treatment Guidelines. It runs on the phones clinicians already carry, works without a signal, and gives contextual guidance at the point of care :  not a generic chatbot answer, a reasoned suggestion grounded in the protocols clinicians already follow.

**First principles (label + one-line definition each):**
1. **Context** :  Guidance that reflects the setting it's given in, not a one-size answer.
2. **Ease** :  Built for the phone and the pace a clinician already has, not one they need to learn.
3. **Integrability** :  Designed to work alongside existing systems, not replace them outright.
4. **Infrastructure** :  Built assuming unreliable power and connectivity, because that's the reality.
5. **Auditability** :  Every suggestion can be traced back to what it was based on.
6. **Speed** :  Answers fast enough to be useful mid-consultation, not after.
7. **Precision** :  Correct over impressive. A stiffer right answer beats a fluent wrong one.

## `/research`

**Intro line:**
> Some of what we build is a finished product. Some of it is still an open question. This page is the open questions.

**Sema section:**
> Small AI models are strong in English and weak in Swahili :  and fine-tuning your way out of that gap isn't realistic on the hardware most clinics can afford. Sema, built with Nama Labs, takes a different approach: instead of translating, it anchors Swahili input to a structured form a small model can already reason over, then renders the answer back using controlled bilingual templates rather than generated prose. It's deliberately unglamorous, and that's the point :  the failure mode is visible instead of silently wrong.
>
> Read the full writeup on Medium → [link]
> Main repository → [github.com/Nama-ResearchLab/Sema]

**Rafiki section:**
> Rafiki is a digital twin training framework, built in collaboration with Nama Labs, for edge-native systems that need to learn from the environment they're deployed in rather than a lab dataset. It's active, ongoing work.
>
> Repository → [github.com/ClerQ-Intelligence-CQ/Rafiki]

## `/about`

**Why ClerQ exists:**
> Care in Tanzania doesn't fail for lack of clinicians trying. It fails at the gaps :  between what a guideline says and what's practical at 2am with no signal, between one system and the next, between a language a tool was built in and the language a patient actually speaks. ClerQ exists to close a few of those gaps, honestly and one at a time, rather than promise to close all of them at once.
