The problem we solve

Tanzania has roughly 1 physician per 10,000 people. Most clinical encounters happen at dispensaries and health centres staffed by clinical officers and assistant medical officers working with limited diagnostic equipment, inconsistent drug supply, and unreliable connectivity. The national Standard Treatment Guidelines exist, but they live in thick printed manuals that are rarely at hand during a consultation.

Meanwhile, most digital health tools are built for contexts that don't match this reality: they assume stable internet, English-only input, server-side processing, and clinicians with time to learn new workflows. When these tools arrive in a rural Tanzanian dispensary, they fail to load or add documentation burden to busy clinics.

That gap between what exists and what's needed is where ClerQ operates.

Clinical Decision Support Grounded in STG 2026

ClerQ CDS is an offline Clinical Decision Support System built for frontline clinical practice in Tanzania. Grounded in the 6th Edition Standard Treatment Guidelines (STG 2026) and NEMLIT, it executes deterministically on the standard smartphones clinicians already carry. No cloud dependency, no internet requirement, no dedicated hardware.

When a clinician encounters a patient, ClerQ assists with structured decision support at the point of care: verifying contraindications, calculating severity scores, and retrieving protocol-recommended management paths in the clinician's working language.

Meet Lucy: The Clinical Assistant

Lucy is the frontline interface of ClerQ CDS. Built specifically for consultation-speed lookup, Lucy accepts clinical inquiries in English or Swahili and retrieves grounded, auditable guidance within seconds, assisting clinician judgment without slowing down high-volume outpatient clinics.

Lucy avoids generative guesswork. Dosages, administration routes, and contraindications are retrieved deterministically from national STG 2026 tables and NEMLIT. Every recommendation cites protocol sections and page references for independent clinician verification.

Hakiki: Declarative Verification for National Clinical Guidelines

Before clinical guidance is loaded onto a clinician's phone, it is verified against national protocols through Hakiki. Hakiki audits symptom weights, clinical thresholds, and drug contraindications directly against Tanzania's STG 2026 and NEMLIT, chapter by chapter, with line-by-line page citations.

When national guidelines update, we update declarative data schemas directly on device. Hakiki also exposes an auditable Model Context Protocol (MCP) server for clinical review and verification.

Declarative Rule Schemas

Clinical protocols are versioned datasets mapped verbatim to official STG 2026 chapters and page numbers for transparent governance.

Deterministic Bar-Crossing

Threshold scoring uses deterministic clinical criteria grounded in peer-reviewed protocols. Red-flag findings saturate immediately.

Clinical MCP Server

Decouples guideline logic from UI layers. Authorised tools query the STG 2026 graph via MCP for expert physician verification.

How it works

A clinician's workflow with ClerQ is designed to add zero friction to existing practice.

01

Patient presents

A clinical officer at a dispensary sees a patient with a presenting complaint. They open ClerQ on the phone they already carry.

02

Ask Lucy

They describe the clinical scenario to Lucy in English or Swahili. Natural language, no structured forms, no dropdown menus. Just the clinical question as they'd describe it to a colleague.

03

Grounded response

Lucy returns a guideline-grounded suggestion within seconds. The response includes the recommended management approach, relevant drug dosages from the STG, and a direct reference to the source guideline section so the clinician can verify independently.

04

Treat with confidence

The clinician uses the suggestion alongside their own clinical judgment. ClerQ doesn't replace the clinician. It gives them the right information at the right time so they can make better-informed decisions under real-world constraints.

Made with purpose

ClerQ is engineered with deliberate purpose to transform African healthcare, starting in Tanzania with a focus on real frontline clinical reality. We begin by solving the hardest operational constraints first: offline execution, low-resource language processing, and strict guideline alignment, establishing a foundation of trust before expanding across the continent.

Language-first

Most AI tools work well in English and poorly in other languages. ClerQ treats Swahili as a primary clinical language, engineered directly from Tanzania's linguistic context. Our Sema pipeline ensures Swahili inputs receive high-fidelity clinical reasoning alongside English.

Infrastructure-honest

We don't build for the infrastructure we wish existed. We build for the infrastructure that's actually there: intermittent power, shared mobile devices, 2G connectivity in the best case, and nothing at all in the common case. ClerQ works fully offline because that's not an edge case in rural clinics. It's the default.

Guideline-grounded

ClerQ's clinical knowledge base is grounded directly in Tanzania's Standard Treatment Guidelines and NEMLIT. The system reflects available essential medicines and realistic facility capabilities across dispensary levels.
